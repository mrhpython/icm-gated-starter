# One agent, a folder, and a second reader

## The part that goes wrong

The draft read fine. The number in the second paragraph was invented. The citation did not exist.
The only reader before it went out was the thing that wrote it, and it was confident.

That is the failure the folder-system idea does not touch. One agent with a map, a desk, and tidy
numbered stages is easier to follow than a swarm, and it still ships whatever it wrote, because
nothing between the draft and the world is looking with different eyes.

## What is in the folder

```
icm-gated-starter/
├── CLAUDE.md          the map: read first, sends the agent to the desk
├── CONTEXT.md         the desk: the job, the decisions, where we left off
├── SETUP.md           one page
├── MAKE-IT-MINE.md    say the words and the started project becomes yours
├── RECEIPTS.md        what we can show, what we do not claim, the gate runs on this text
└── stages/
    ├── 01-plan/       the brief, already written
    ├── 02-draft/      half written; your first job is to finish it
    ├── 03-verify/     the gate, the banned-claims grep, the mutant that must fail
    └── 04-live/       publishing, which is yours
```

The map, the desk, and the numbered stages are Jake Van Clief and David McDermott's Interpretable
Context Methodology. The last two folders are what we add.

## Why a verify stage

The missing piece is a reader that is not the writer. The verify stage is that reader.

It runs an independent checker, and independent means four things you can check for yourself. It is
a separate model from the one that wrote the draft. Its checks are fixed, the same set on every run.
Its findings are written out where you can open them and argue with them. And when a check cannot
complete, the result is UNKNOWN, not a pass, so a checker that did not run is never mistaken for a
checker that found nothing.

The checker can run locally against a local model at no cost. There is also a hosted demo endpoint at
api.soulfield.one that needs no key, good for a few calls a day per address.

Two more things sit beside the checker, because the checker alone is not enough. The gate does not
know which claims you have ruled out for your own project; the grep does, from a list you write. And
the mutant: a copy of the draft with defects seeded into it on purpose. It goes through the same gate
as the real draft, and it must fail every run. If it ever passes, the gate is not trusted, whatever it
said about the real draft.

We did not add this on principle. Our own single-agent drafts shipped defects that a staged line with
a gate later caught. That is the receipt, and it is in RECEIPTS.md in the folder.

The verify stage is not a person reading the draft. The person's seat is the last stage, publishing,
and it is kept.

## What we do not claim

There is no number on this page for how well the gate does on your material. We have not run it on
your material, so we do not know, and we say so rather than estimate.

We could not show that staging alone beats a bare draft. What we can show is narrower: a staged line
with a gate caught defects our single-agent drafts had shipped. We publish the narrower result and
the thing we could not show side by side, rather than leaving the second one out.

A pass is not clearance. A pass means the checks ran and reported nothing, and the mutant failed as
it should. It does not mean the page is true. It means the checks that ran did not find it false.

Nothing in this kit publishes. Stage 04 is a handback, every time, and a person owns it.

## Get it

The kit is a public repository under Apache-2.0. It needs no email address.

Clone icm-gated-starter. Read SETUP.md, which is one page. Finish the draft that is waiting in stage
02, run it through stage 03, and stop at 04. Then run one week of your real work through the same
line: the pages, emails, and documents you would have shipped anyway.

Keep that week's gate results. They are evidence about your material, which is the one thing this
page cannot give you.
